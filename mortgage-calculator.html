How to use the Mortgage Calculator

Enter the home price, down payment, interest rate and loan term, plus yearly taxes, insurance and any HOA fee if you know them. The tool works out the monthly payment, split into principal and interest, with taxes, insurance and HOA added on top, which is the PITI figure lenders often quote. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises the total interest.

Example

A 300,000 home with a 60,000 down payment leaves a 240,000 loan. At 6 percent over 30 years, principal and interest alone are about 1,439 a month. Add taxes, insurance and HOA to get the real monthly cost. This is an estimate for planning only: closing costs, rate changes and lender fees are not included, so confirm the final figures with a lender.

Fixed or variable rates

A fixed interest rate stays the same for an agreed period, which makes monthly payments predictable, while a variable rate can rise or fall with the market, which can lower payments but also raise them. When comparing offers, look at the total cost over the full term, not just the first monthly payment, and ask about fees, early repayment charges and what happens when a fixed period ends. A mortgage adviser can explain which option fits your income and plans.
